# Design brief

Reader: an agent handing finished requirements on to a design.

## When

- The requirements are written and a design note or work issue is to be built from them.
- An idea arrives with its design already attached and needs to be cut down to what holds.

## Steps

1. Measured today: write what the tree does now, measured on the base branch, with the command
   and its output. No claim about today rests on memory.
2. The strongest case against: write the best argument for not doing the work, or for doing it
   another way, as its advocate would.
3. What survives: write the part of the design that holds after the case against, and why it
   holds.
4. Deferred, with its trigger: write each part left out, each with the measurement that would turn
   it into work, such as a count, a time or a failure seen twice.

## Where it goes in SE

The four parts map onto the work issue: what is measured today is its Situation section, the
case against and what survives are its Approach section with the options not picked, and each
deferred part with its measurement is named there as later work. A choice left between survivors
goes to the decision skill.

## Source

quackitect v4's lesson of one core with many satellites (read): a design keeps the small core
that survives and defers the rest, each part with the measure that would bring it in. Gary Klein,
"Performing a Project Premortem", Harvard Business Review, 2007, for arguing the case against.
